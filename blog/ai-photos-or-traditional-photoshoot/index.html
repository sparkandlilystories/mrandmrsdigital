AI photos or a traditional photoshoot? How to choose

An honest guide for small brands: when a real shoot is worth it, when AI is the smarter spend, and how to get the best of both.

by Mrs Digital10 November 20266 min read

Choose a traditional photoshoot when you need large batches of plain packshots, real people wearing or using the product, or texture that sells the product on its own. Choose AI for lifestyle and campaign scenes, seasonal refreshes and testing ideas, where a shoot gets expensive. Many small brands get the best value by mixing the two.

It is the question almost every small brand reaches sooner or later: should I book a photographer or go with AI? We sell AI images, so you might expect a one-word answer. The honest one is longer, because the right choice depends on what your images need to do.

The real difference between AI product photography and a traditional photoshoot is where your product has to be: in front of a camera on the day, or only in a few clear photos that an AI model then places into new scenes.

What is the real difference between the two?

A photoshoot captures your real product in a real place, with a photographer, a set and often a stylist or model, all booked for the same day. AI product photography starts from clear photos of your product and builds the scene around it digitally. One captures a moment that happened. The other creates a moment that didn’t need to.

That single difference explains most of the trade-offs. A shoot needs the product, the people and the light in one place, so it costs time to organise and money every time you want something new. AI only needs the product once, so a new season, colour or setting is a new image, not a new booking. In exchange, someone has to check every AI image closely, because the model can drift from the real product.

When is a traditional photoshoot the better choice?

Book a photographer when you need a large batch of plain white-background packshots, when real people must wear or use the product, or when texture is the whole selling point. A good photographer can work through 20 to 40 simple products a day,3 and real models show fit and movement in a way customers trust completely.

Clothing fit is the clearest example. If a customer buys a dress because of how it sits on the model, that drape has to be real. The UK advertising rules apply to AI images exactly as they do to photos, and an image that misrepresents what a product does or looks like is misleading, however it was made.4

When does AI make more sense?

AI makes more sense for lifestyle and campaign images, where a shoot would need a location, props, styling and good weather. It also suits seasonal refreshes, new colourways and testing several looks before you commit. The product never has to travel, and a new scene doesn’t mean a new booking, which matters when your budget is small.

It is also the faster route when you need variety. The same product can sit on a Christmas table in one image and on a sunny terrace in the next, which is exactly what keeps a shop and a social feed from looking repetitive.

How do the costs compare?

A UK photographer typically charges £15 to £30 per white-background image, or £425 to £500 for a half-day lifestyle shoot, before studio hire, props and a model. With an average West London studio at £75 an hour, a half day comes to about £725. At our studio an AI product image starts at £35 and a campaign set at £350.

Those figures come from Airtasker’s UK cost guide and Tagvenue’s studio listings.1,2 The pattern is simple: for plain packshots, a photographer often wins on price; the more a scene needs, the more AI saves. The full cost guide has every number side by side.

Can I use both?

Yes, and for many small brands it is the smartest option. Book one simple shoot for clean, accurate packshots of your range, then use those photos as the reference for AI lifestyle and campaign scenes. You pay for the studio once, and every new season, colour or campaign is built from images you already own.

Good packshots also make AI images more faithful, because the model has sharp, true-to-life detail to work from. It is the closest thing to having a photographer on call without paying for a set every month.

How do I decide for my next launch?

Ask yourself four questions. Do real people need to wear or hold it? How many products need plain shots? Do you need scenes that would mean travel, props or styling? How often will you need fresh images? If people and volume dominate, book a shoot. If scenes and frequency dominate, AI will usually serve you better.

If the answers are mixed, start with the hybrid approach above. It is easier to add AI scenes to good packshots than to fix a campaign that was shot in the wrong place.

Questions we hear a lot

Will customers be able to tell my product photos are AI?

Not if they are made well. The giveaways are usually a changed label, a plastic-looking surface or light that doesn’t match the scene, and all three can be checked and fixed before an image goes live.

Can AI show my clothing or shoes being worn?

It can, but this is where accuracy matters most. Fit, length and fabric drape must match the real item, or customers will feel misled when the parcel arrives. For fit-critical products, many brands keep a real model for the main shop images.

Can you work from my photographer’s packshots?

Yes. Clear, well-lit packshots are an excellent starting point, and they help us keep every detail of the product exact in the new scenes.
