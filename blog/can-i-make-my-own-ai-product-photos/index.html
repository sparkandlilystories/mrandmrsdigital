Can I make my own AI product photos?

Yes, and you can start today with tools you may already have. Here is how, where it goes wrong, and when a studio is worth paying for.

by Mrs Digital6 November 20267 min read

Yes. With a sharp phone photo and a free or low-cost tool like Shopify’s built-in image editor or ChatGPT, you can put your product into a new background in minutes. Simple shop images are very doable on your own. It gets harder when the label must stay perfect, the scene must look real, or you need a whole matching set.

Before we ever had a paying client, I learnt this exactly the way you would: typing descriptions into an AI tool and seeing what came back. No design background, no Photoshop, lots of strange results. So this guide is the one I wish I had then, written for brand owners rather than AI hobbyists.

An AI product photo is an image of your real product placed into a new scene by an AI image model, using your own photos of the product as the starting point.

Can I really make AI product photos myself?

Yes. If you can take a clear photo of your product and describe the scene you want, you can make a usable AI product image yourself. Background swaps and simple styled shots are the easiest place to start. You don’t need to draw or edit, but you do need patience and a sharp eye for detail.

The skill that matters is describing. You tell the tool what to keep (your product, exactly as it is) and what to change (the surface, the light, the mood). Most of the work is in the checking afterwards, which we will come to.

Which AI tools can a small brand start with?

If you sell on Shopify, start with its built-in media generation, which can swap or generate backgrounds and is currently free on all plans. ChatGPT also edits uploaded photos, with limited image generation on its free plan and more on paid plans. Midjourney is popular for striking imagery, but it has no free trial.

  • Shopify Magic media generation: background removal, new backgrounds from a short prompt and extended edges, inside your Shopify admin. Free for a limited time on all plans. Images come out at about 1 megapixel and carry an invisible watermark that doesn’t restrict commercial use.1
  • ChatGPT: upload your product photo and describe the new scene in plain English. The free plan includes limited image generation; Plus is around £20 a month in the UK.2
  • Midjourney: plans start at $10 a month, with no free trial.3 It is built for creating new imagery from words, so keeping your exact product unchanged takes more work.

Don’t spend a week comparing tools. Pick the one you can open today and give it a proper try.

How do I write a prompt for a product photo?

Describe four things: your product and that it must stay unchanged, the surface or setting, the light, and the camera angle. Short and specific beats long and poetic. Then change one thing at a time between attempts, so you can see which word actually made the difference, and keep the prompts that work.

Here is the difference in practice:

  • Weak: “My candle in a nice setting.”
  • Better: “Keep this candle jar and its label exactly as they are. Place it on a cream linen tablecloth next to a sunny window, soft morning light from the left, a dried rose beside it, eye-level close-up, shallow depth of field.”

Shopify’s own guidance adds two useful tips: tell the tool what the product is standing on, or it may look like it is floating, and mention its size so the scale looks right.1

Where do DIY AI product photos usually go wrong?

The product itself is the usual casualty. AI models redraw the whole image, so labels blur, logos change, lids grow new shapes and the product can quietly shrink or float. The other giveaways are plastic-looking surfaces, impossible shadows and hands with the wrong number of fingers. Every one of these is easy to miss on a phone screen.

Before you post anything, put the AI image next to a photo of the real product and check, in this order:

  1. the label and logo, letter by letter
  2. the shape, colour and size of the product
  3. the shadow under it, and anything touching it

If a customer would be surprised when the parcel arrives, the image isn’t ready.

Is it allowed to sell with AI product photos?

Yes, as long as the image doesn’t mislead. In the UK there is currently no rule that says you must label AI images in ads, but the usual advertising rules apply in full. The Committee of Advertising Practice warns that an AI image showing results a product can’t deliver is misleading, and an “AI-generated” label doesn’t fix that.

The example CAP gives is a cosmetic product shown with effects that don’t match real-world results.4 The same thinking applies to a mug that looks bigger than it is, or a fabric that looks softer than it feels. A beautiful scene is fine. A changed product isn’t. This is general information rather than legal advice, so check anything borderline before you spend money on ads.

When is it worth paying an AI studio instead?

Pay a studio when your time is worth more than the images, when the product has fine detail that must stay perfect, or when you need a matching set for a launch or campaign. For a few background swaps in your shop, try it yourself first. You will learn what you like, and that makes any brief better.

What should I try first this week?

Take one product and make five images of it. Keep the product and the prompt the same, and change only one thing each time: the surface, the light, the angle, the props, then the mood. By the fifth image you will understand how your tool behaves better than any tutorial could teach you.

Save the prompt that gave you the best result. That one sentence is the start of your brand’s visual style.

Questions we hear a lot

Do I need a good camera to make AI product photos?

No. A recent phone is enough, as long as the photo is sharp, evenly lit and shows the label clearly. Shoot near a window, against a plain background, from the angle you want in the final image.

Can I use free AI tools for my shop images?

Often, yes. Shopify, for example, says it doesn’t restrict where you use images made with its media generation tool. Each tool has its own terms, so check them before you use an image in paid ads.

Do I have to tell customers my product photos are AI?

There is currently no UK rule that says you must label AI use in ads. The rule that matters is that the image must not mislead people about the product, and a label saying it is AI does not fix an image that misleads.

Why does AI keep changing my logo or label?

Image models redraw the whole picture, including your product, so small lettering and logos are the first things to drift. Keep the label large and sharp in your source photo, ask for it to stay unchanged, and always compare the result with the real product.

How long does it take to get a good result on my own?

For a simple background swap, minutes. For a styled lifestyle scene that looks like your product and nothing else, expect plenty of attempts and checking, especially at the start.
